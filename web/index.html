    FAQ

    Answers to the questions that come up most often.

    My pay looks wrong

    Start with Handshake's guide to how AI payments are processed:

    Handshake AI payments processing

    For Ivy and Roadhouse tasking, people are usually told the same thing in Slack: a task counts once it reaches Ready to Deliver or Delivered. Review stages, Awaiting Review, Needs Fixing, and staging are still in the review loop.

    Payments can take about a week to show up, so the dashboard estimate is just a quick check. The Handshake Payments page is still the final source of truth.

    How do I know if a task is RTD or Delivered?

    Use this dashboard first. It pulls the exact stage from your Handshake task list and shows it next to each task.

    On the HAI task page, you can also hover over the task ID in the top-left of a task. Alex told people that Unknown can still mean RTD or Delivered after you hover, so check the hover text before assuming it is stuck.

    If it says Awaiting Review, Review 1, Review 2, staging, or Needs Fixing, it has not fully cleared the review path yet.

    When are new tasks coming?

    Tasks come in batches. They are not assigned to you one-by-one, and there is not a fixed daily schedule.

    When a batch drops, claim tasks yourself from the HAI task list. They can go fast, so quiet days usually just mean the last batch is done and the team is waiting on the next one.

    For updates, check ivy-online-ranking-announcements, roadhouse-announcements, the project notes, and office hours.

    I don't have access to the GitHub repo
    • Fill out the GitHub access form.
    • A scheduler checks the form every 15 minutes and adds people to the task files repo.
    • Check your email and spam folder for the invite, and make sure the username and email you submitted are correct.
    • If you still cannot get in, DM Alex with your GitHub username and email.
    I can't find the task files
    • They are in the ivy-offline folder inside the online repo.
    • Offline tasks use IDs for filenames. Match the task ID to the file in the repo.
    • The offline task hub has more details.
    • If a Roadhouse or Ivy task is too large for the repo, use the GitHub release. The large files people keep looking for are cosco-data-corruption.zip, ninja-cicd-target-determinator.zip, and unintended-adds-reconciliation-v2.zip.
    My task says Needs Fixing but I don't see feedback

    Check your task tab first. Select the task, click View, and look for feedback on the right side. If nothing shows there, check the most recent sheet that was sent by the team.

    If the task still has no feedback, the feedback looks stale, or the task ID is missing from the sheet, ask in ivy-online-ranking-tasking with the task ID and what you checked.

    I want to refer a friend

    DM Rhythm or Nandini with your friend's HAI Fellow ID. Your friend may need to pass up to two assessments, and the software engineering team usually needs another one or two days to review the results.

    New fellows who joined in July or later also need to complete the work-authorization form. Handshake checks it against the information on the HAI platform.

    I want to work on other projects

    Ivy Online is the path into Project Pacemaker and Roadhouse. Before asking to move, make sure you have strong quality on Ivy and a solid record of delivered tasks.

    Coding projects rotate between pilots. Ask the code pod SPL about other coding work. For non-code projects, check the HAI platform.

    I want to be a reviewer or specialist

    You do not need to ask. When more reviewers are needed, the team checks its top fellows automatically. These are the things that improve your chances:

    • Delivering two or three tasks each week.
    • Strong first-pass quality with few major errors or hard blockers.
    • Helping other fellows in the help channel.
    • Finding important blockers or bugs during tasking.
    Work-authorization requirements for Yukon

    You can task on Yukon if one of these applies:

    • You are a US or Canadian citizen.
    • You are a permanent resident of the US or Canada.
    • You have a valid EAD, including DACA, TPS, refugee or asylee status, or H-4/L-2 with an EAD.
    • You are an international student with CPT or OPT approved for this program by your school's DSO.

    You cannot continue if your authorization is tied to a specific employer, including H-1B, TN, J-1, or STEM OPT, or if you do not have US/Canadian work authorization or approved CPT/OPT.

    You must be physically in the US or Canada whenever you task. Get an approved exception before traveling.
